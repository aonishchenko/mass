# MASS — Pitch & Q&A Playbook (v3)
**Format: 4:00 pitch + 3:00 Q&A. Structure: David Beckett (best3minutes) — pain → product-as-outcome → unique → proof → team → close/CTA.**
**Golden rule: first 90 seconds have NO sponsor names and NO tech words. Human problem first. Tech lives in "unique" and Q&A.**

---

## 0. POSITIONING GUARDRAILS — kill the wrong frame in the judge's head

The three misreads that would sink us, and the one-line kill for each. Deliver
the kill-line the SECOND you sense the misread, do not wait for the question.

| If they think we're... | Why it's wrong | KILL-LINE (memorize) |
|---|---|---|
| **Claude / ChatGPT / Gemini** (a chat UI) | Those are ONE human ↔ ONE agent. We are MANY verified humans building & OWNING one agent. Opposite axis. | *"We're not a place to chat with an AI. We're a way to co-own the AI you build."* |
| **Git / GitHub / Cursor / Zed** (dev collab) | Those version CODE. We attribute KNOWLEDGE and distribute OWNERSHIP. We never touch a repo. | *"Git tracks who changed a file. We track who taught an agent — and pay them for it."* |
| **Notion / Miro / Taskade** (a workspace) | Those help you DO work. We mint an owned, EARNING asset. | *"A workspace helps you do work. MASS builds you a coworker you own."* |

**The single identity sentence (repeat till it sticks):**
> "MASS is how a team builds an AI team member together and provably co-owns what it becomes."

**The novelty in one breath (for the tired judge):**
> "Everyone can build AI agents. Nobody can prove who taught one what, or share what it earns with the people who made it valuable. We do."

**Why this is a REAL problem, not invented (say if challenged on "so what"):**
> "AI is eating knowledge work, and the people whose expertise makes an agent good get erased and unpaid. That's the fairness problem of the agent economy. We make contribution provable and ownable."

### The crystal-clear explainer (for a NON-technical judge, no jargon at all)
Use the band analogy. It lands in 15 seconds with anyone:
> "Think of a band recording an album. Everyone who played on it is credited, and
> everyone gets royalties when it sells. Now think of a team teaching an AI
> everything it knows. Today: no credits, no royalties, and one person owns the
> account. MASS gives that AI a proper credits list and a royalty split — so the
> people who taught it own it."

Ladder of explanations — pick the rung that matches who's asking:
1. **10 words:** "Credits and royalties for the humans who teach an AI."
2. **One sentence:** "A team builds an AI coworker together, and everyone who taught it provably owns a share of what it earns."
3. **Three sentences:** add *how* — verified humans, an immutable record of who taught what, automatic payouts.
4. **Technical:** the four-partner architecture (only if they ask).

**The 'why now' line (if asked why this didn't exist before):**
> "Two things only became possible recently: proving someone is a unique human
> without exposing who they are, and paying tiny amounts automatically to many
> people. Without both, contribution ownership can't work."

## 1. THE 4-MINUTE SCRIPT (second-counts + demo cues)

> "The most valuable worker on almost any team today is an AI agent. And here's

## 3. EXHAUSTIVE Q&A BANK (3 min live; expect 4–6 asked — prepare ALL)
*Each: a tight spoken answer; where marked [SHOW], a live click. A clicked answer beats a spoken one.*

### A. "ISN'T THIS JUST X" — most likely, most dangerous. Lead every answer by RE-STATING the axis.
- **Collaborative ChatGPT / a Claude or OpenAI chat UI?**
  → "Those are one human using one agent. We're many verified humans building and
  *owning* one agent. The product isn't the chat — it's provable ownership and
  attribution of what you build. Different axis entirely."
- **The chat window looks like ChatGPT though.**
  → "The chat is just the doorway. Everything that matters happens around it:
  who's verified, what gets accepted into the brain, who owns the result. Strip
  the chat and the product is still there — an on-chain cap table for an agent."
- **Git / GitHub / Cursor / Zed for agents?**
  → "Git tracks who edited code. We track who taught an agent *judgment*, and turn
  that into a cap table that pays out. Knowledge and ownership, not files. We
  never touch a repo."
- **A workspace like Notion / Miro / Taskade with AI bolted on?**
  → "Those help you do work. We mint an owned, earning asset — a coworker with a
  cap table. The output is ownership, not a document."
- **OKX / Virtuals already run agent marketplaces — how are you different?**
  → "They let *finished* agents transact. We're upstream: how an agent gets *built*
  by a team and how that team *co-owns* it. You could list our agent on them —
  that's roadmap, not competition."
- **Foundry on 0G already does co-owned agents — clone?**
  → "Foundry pools *capital and data* into models. We pool *verified human labor*,
  one accepted contribution at a time, sybil-proof. Capital co-ownership vs.
  contribution co-ownership. Different input."
- **Story / OpenLedger do attribution + royalties already.**
  → "On *training data*, at model scale. We do it on *interactive contributions*,
  at session granularity, gated by verified unique humans. They're the macro
  layer; we're the team layer."
- **Mem0 / memory startups track contribution too.**
  → "In-app, off-chain, no economics. Ours is on-chain, sybil-proof, and pays
  out. Provenance you can *own*, not just view."
- **The blunt one: what's actually NEW?**
  → "Three things, precisely: sybil-proof contribution equity, live quorum
  authority, and an agent that cites its human teachers. Each unshipped
  elsewhere, as far as we've found."

### B. THE BRAIN — credibility landmine, rehearse verbatim
- **Are you training / fine-tuning a model?**
  → "No — and we're careful to say so. The 'brain' is curated, encrypted memory,
  instructions, and skills on 0G. Not weights. 0G's fine-tuning is live and it's
  our natural next step, but we're not claiming it today."
- **So the 'learning' is really retrieval / context?**
  → "Yes — curated context with provenance. The novelty isn't the learning
  method, it's that every piece is attributed to a verified human and
  economically binding."
- **Then couldn't anyone build this with a vector DB?**
  → "The retrieval is the easy 10%. The hard, novel 90% is: verified-human
  attribution, live quorum authority, sealed screening, and an on-chain cap table
  that pays out. That's the product."

### C. SECURITY / ABUSE — shows maturity (0G-flavored)
- **What stops someone teaching it something malicious?**
  → Immune system: each proposed contribution is screened by a sealed inference
  before acceptance; verdict logged. [SHOW screened verdict in ticker.]
- **Prompt injection via a contribution?**
  → Same screening gate; contributions are data, not executable instructions to
  the tool layer.
- **Poisoning the cap table with junk contributions?**
  → Acceptance needs a 2-of-M verified-human co-sign; junk never reaches the log.
- **A verified human leaves mid-session?**
  → Authority recomputes instantly; the agent loses whatever their presence
  unlocked; in-flight actions complete-then-lock.
- **Sybil attack on ownership?**
  → Every share traces to a World-verified unique human + a sybil score; that's
  the whole point of the verification layer.

### D. LEGAL / TOKENOMICS — YOUR HOME TURF, answer with authority
- **Isn't the crew token a security?**

  schedule — not a revenue-share instrument. Structuring exactly these
  distinctions is my day job." *(Lean in, don't hedge.)*
- **How is the cap table calculated?**
  → "Count of accepted contributions per verified human in the HCS log. Every
  mint references the underlying log entries — fully auditable." [SHOW]
- **Who legally owns the agent?**

- **What about disputes over contribution value?**
  → "v1 counts accepted contributions equally — deliberately simple and

  auditable."
- **GDPR / personal data in the log?**
  → "The log stores hashes, not content or identities; plaintext stays encrypted

### K. "WHY BLOCKCHAIN AT ALL?" — the hardest generalist question. Never get defensive.
- **Why does this need a blockchain? Couldn't it be a database?**
  → "Because the whole product is a *promise to strangers*: your share is real and
  nobody can quietly edit it. A company database can be changed by whoever runs it.
  Three things genuinely need neutral rails: a tamper-proof record of who taught
  what, ownership that survives us shutting down, and automatic payouts to many
  people. Take those away and there's no product."
- **So it's a database with extra steps?**
  → "The test is: would you accept a share in something where the owner can edit
  the ledger and switch off the payouts? Nobody would. That's the difference."
- **Why not just use Stripe for the payouts?**
  → "Fine for five people in one country. Not for many small automatic splits
  across borders, machine-speed, sub-cent. And Stripe can't prove the ledger
  behind the split wasn't edited."
- **What if crypto disappeared tomorrow?**
  → "The idea — credits and royalties for AI contributors — survives; the rails
  would change. But today these rails are the only neutral ones that exist."

## 4. DELIVERY REMINDERS (Beckett)
- Pain first, ALWAYS. Catch yourself opening with a sponsor name → stop.
- Product = result, not mechanism. "They'll ask how" — and they will, in Q&A.
- One demo beat in the body (citation), one at the close (Birth + First Job).
- Team = weapon. Slow down. Certainty and passion.
- CTA unmistakable: "hire our agent yourself."
- The moment you sense a wrong-frame ("oh it's collaborative ChatGPT"), fire the
  kill-line from section 0 immediately.
- Live demo risky? Backup recording queued; narrate calmly over it.
- Who speaks: decide now. One voice drives the 4-min narrative; the builder
  fields deep technical Q&A. Don't both answer at once.
- Match the explanation to the asker: use the band analogy for generalists, the
  three claims for technical judges. Never open with architecture.
- If a question has a grain of truth, AGREE with that grain first, then reframe.
  Defensiveness reads as weakness; calm agreement reads as command of the problem.
